Multi-Brand Hospitality

Building a B2B Pipeline From Scratch (When the Tech Stack Fights You Every Step of the Way)

One BDM doing manual cold outreach, a CRM that doesn't support automation, and an events calendar with gaps to fill. I built the system that tied it all together.

Marketing Automation B2B Pipeline Lead Nurture CRM Integration Change Management

The Situation

The company operates a large event and meeting venue within a hospitality portfolio. The events calendar had gaps that needed filling, and the business development team consisted of one person doing manual cold outreach to event planners.

She's great at researching companies and finding opportunities. But everything after that was manual. Every follow-up, every touchpoint, every nurture email was her, writing and sending one at a time.

Leadership came to me with two separate asks. The first: help her scale. The second: what else can marketing do to fill the calendar? I saw them as one problem. We had no automated pipeline. Everything depended on manual effort. And there was an entire pool of warm and lapsed contacts sitting untouched in the CRM with zero follow-up happening.

The Strategy

I designed two parallel systems that fed into the same goal: filling the events calendar through automated, segmented outreach.

System 1
Reactivation Engine
Targeted contacts already in our database who had gone cold. Three distinct segments, each with different messaging and cadence.
System 2
Outreach Automation
Built around the BDM's existing research workflow. Zero disruption to how she works. The system picks up where her manual process used to start.

The reactivation engine targeted three segments already sitting in the database:

Segment 1
Past Clients
"We'd love to have you back."
Segment 2
Lost RFPs
"We'd love another shot."
Segment 3
Unconverted Site Visits
"Still looking for a venue?"

The outreach automation was built around the BDM's existing workflow. She was already filling out a research document for every prospect she identified. I kept that intact. The system picks up where her manual process used to start: it follows up automatically in her voice based on the data she gathered, watches for signs of engagement, and when a lead warms up, it kicks them back to her for personal follow-up.

On top of both systems, I'm running display ads on Google and LinkedIn as air cover, targeting the specific contacts we're reaching out to. So when a prospect gets an email and then sees our venue in a display ad that same week, it reinforces the touchpoint without us spending on broad awareness.

The Obstacles

The Problem
The company uses Amadeus Delphi as their CRM, which doesn't support marketing automation natively. There's no built-in way to trigger email sequences, segment contacts by behavior, or track engagement.
The Solve
I built the entire automation layer from Zapier and Mailchimp. Zapier handles the internal logic, moving contacts between stages based on tags and statuses. Mailchimp handles the actual communication.
The Problem
The reactivation engine depends on knowing when someone does a site visit, and the sales team wasn't tracking that.
The Solve
I had to make the case for why tracking matters, train them on the process, and follow up consistently until the behavior stuck. That's not a technical problem. That's a change management problem, and it took more effort than building the automation itself.

The System Design

The architecture I built connects three layers:

Data Layer
The BDM's research feeds the outreach system. The sales team's CRM updates feed the reactivation system. Both flow through Zapier, which handles the routing logic.
Communication Layer
Mailchimp sends the actual emails, personalized by segment and triggered by status changes. Each sequence has its own cadence, messaging, and escalation rules.
Handoff Layer
Engagement signals (opens, clicks, replies) are monitored. Warm leads get flagged and routed back for personal follow-up. Cold leads move to long-term nurture. The system sorts itself over time.
The whole point was to build something that works without me babysitting it. The BDM keeps doing what she's good at (research and relationships), the system handles the repetitive middle, and the pipeline fills itself.
