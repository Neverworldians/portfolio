When Rankings Go Up But Leads Go Down: Diagnosing a Conversion Crisis

A plastic surgery client's SEO was winning on every metric except the one that mattered. I found the real problem, sold the fix, and delivered the best year in the clinic's history.

SEO CRO Website Redesign Strategic Consulting

The Problem

This was a long-standing plastic surgery and medspa client with a legacy website built around 2014–2015, well before modern UX, CRO, or mobile expectations. I inherited the SEO account years later, and for a while, performance was strong.

But over the course of several months in 2023–2024, I noticed a steady and significant decline in leads, even as rankings and traffic continued to climb.

Rankings up. Traffic up. Leads down. Something in the middle was broken, and the surface metrics were hiding it.

The Diagnosis

I started where most people skip: the data.

1
Funnel Analysis Root cause found
Reviewed internal dashboards and call tracking metrics. Phone leads, previously the top conversion type, had dropped dramatically. Form fills were steady. The bleed was coming from calls.
2
Technical Audit All clear
Full audit to rule out obvious issues: broken CTAs, mobile click-to-call failures, form glitches. Nothing was malfunctioning. The site worked. It just wasn't working for users.
3
Traffic & Ranking Verification All clear
Confirmed we were not losing traffic. On the contrary, we were attracting more, including local traffic. Rankings for core procedures remained stable or improved. This was not a visibility problem.
4
Competitive SERP Review Problem confirmed
Investigated how we stacked up in the actual search landscape for top terms. Every competitor had modern, clean websites with updated branding and more persuasive content. Our client's site looked dated, text-heavy, and visually cluttered by comparison.
We had a conversion bottleneck hidden beneath surface-level success metrics. We were winning the race to get people to the site, then losing them the moment they arrived.

The Business Case

I built and delivered a strategic presentation walking the client through exactly how competitors were winning conversions with better first impressions, not better rankings. I showed the data trail: traffic up, rankings up, leads down, and what the competitive landscape looked like side by side.

He approved a full rebrand and website redesign.

The Execution

I led the project across three teams: coordinating with designers and the creative director on design direction (balancing brand identity with SEO/conversion performance), directing the web development team through the build, and leading the content workstream directly.

→
Content Audit & Cleanup
Audited and removed thin, outdated legacy content that predated my involvement. Reduced bloat and simplified the migration.
→
Page Migration & Content Refresh
Led copywriters through key pages being rewritten or heavily edited with updated USPs, tighter copy, and clearer CTAs. Some content was merged to improve structure. Pages that performed well were migrated intact, preserving SEO equity.
→
Risk Management
Communicated upfront that there would be an initial traffic dip post-redesign, which is standard for migrations. Setting that expectation early meant the client didn't panic when the dip happened and trusted the recovery plan.

The Results

The traffic dip came as expected, followed by a strong recovery. New pages began ranking higher than before, including keywords we'd been historically stuck outside the top 3 for.
