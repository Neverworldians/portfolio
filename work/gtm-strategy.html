Multi-Brand Hospitality · Pre-Launch

Building the Go-to-Market Strategy for a Lifestyle Brand Launch

A hospitality company wanted to become a lifestyle brand. I designed the strategic framework that answered the question: okay, we have a brand. Now how does it actually work?

Go-to-Market Strategy Brand Architecture Loyalty Design Content Strategy Audience Architecture

Note: Brand name and identifying details have been anonymized due to a pre-launch NDA. The strategic framework, positioning, and architecture are my original work, approved by leadership and currently in implementation.

The Situation

The company operates a multi-brand hospitality portfolio: restaurants, a hotel, a pool club, event spaces, and meeting venues. Leadership had a long-term vision to evolve beyond hospitality and into a lifestyle brand that could eventually sell products (wellness, sleep, home goods) and scale to other cities.

The problem was that the existing properties served completely different audiences with no connective tissue between them. There was no shared database, no cross-property awareness, and no mechanism to turn a one-time guest into a long-term brand relationship.

Through early working sessions with the VP of marketing, we landed on a loyalty program as the connective tissue between properties. That loyalty program needed to live somewhere, which is how the app came into the picture: it was the infrastructure for building and owning the database.

A creative director was hired to build the brand identity. My job was to answer the question that came after: okay, we have a brand and a $1.6M budget. Now make it work.

On-Property Conversion (The Critical Bridge)
Every guest touchpoint (receipt QR codes, table tents, server prompts, WiFi capture) drives into the brand platform. QR directs to a lightweight web experience for immediate point redemption, which then promotes the native app download. This reduces friction at the highest-intent moment: when the guest is still on property and just had a positive experience.

The Loyalty Program

The app needed to offer immediate, tangible value beyond "we're a cool brand." I designed a three-layer loyalty structure:

🪙
Earn
Points system where guests earn by dining, attending events, and engaging with the brand. Traditional, but the lowest barrier to entry.
🎁
Spend
Redeem points at our properties and at curated local partner businesses: boutiques, fitness studios, coffee shops, bookstores handpicked for brand alignment.
💛
Give
A portion of every redemption supports a local community cause, with the option to donate points directly.
This solved a real cold-start problem. At launch, we don't have enough of our own properties, products, or events to fill out an app on our own. So we curate the neighborhood, build the database, and create a habit of checking the app. As we grow, the ratio shifts. The local partners go from being the main attraction to the seasoning.

Social Teasers
Email Newsletters

Paid Amplification

SEO acquisition: topics like "what to do in LA this weekend" where we highlight local partners and our own events. Gets eyeballs from people who don't know the brand exists yet.

Editorial depth: profiles on local artists, stories behind our partners, the inspiration behind our spaces. This gives the brand a point of view and makes people want to stay once they find us.

Both types of content become fuel for every other channel. Social pulls teasers. Email curates highlights. PR coverage generates backlinks and builds AI search visibility. Paid boosts top performers.

Audience Architecture

I developed detailed behavioral audience profiles, segmented into four groups. These aren't demographic buckets. They're behavioral scenarios: the specific situations and motivations that drive someone to discover and engage with a property. Click to explore each one.

Acquisition
Experience Seekers
▼
Locals
Someone who wants to impress a date.
Someone who wants a girls night that looks good on Instagram stories.
Someone bored of their usual spots who wants something new this weekend.
Travelers
Someone visiting who wants to eat where locals eat, not where tourists go.
Someone on a work trip with one free evening who wants to make it count.
Acquisition
Celebrators
▼
Locals

Someone looking for an anniversary dinner that feels elevated.
Someone planning a proposal who needs the setting to be perfect.
Travelers
Someone planning a destination bachelorette who wants everything walkable.
Someone doing a couples getaway who wants the whole experience handled.
Acquisition
Casual Convenience
▼
Locals
Someone who wants pool-side drinks without planning a vacation.
Someone who needs a last-minute dinner reservation and doesn't want to think too hard.
Travelers
Someone staying at the hotel who wants dinner without leaving the property.
Someone who just checked in and wants food immediately without researching options.
Retention · Brand Advocates
Early Adopters
▼
Someone who likes discovering new brands before they blow up.
Someone who wants to feel like an insider with access to perks and events.
Someone who would buy merch from a hospitality brand if it was actually cool.

Each acquisition segment was further split into Locals and Travelers, because a local planning a bachelorette and a traveler looking for a one-night dinner recommendation need completely different messaging, content, and channel strategies. These profiles drove content strategy, ad targeting, SEO keyword priorities, and the app's feature set.
